1st place: a safety standard for space tourism

Brandon Hall, Aanya Vegad and I proposed the first international passenger safety standard for commercial spaceflight, modelled on Virgin Galactic as the first certified carrier. We won the consulting track and advance to the Ontario Engineering Competition 2027.

Result1st place · to OEC 2027

The story

Two weekends, one of the most popular streams

We signed up for the Engineering Consulting stream close to the deadline and only later realised it was one of the competition's most popular streams, running at full capacity. It was also far more work than we expected: over two weekends we produced a 21-page report and a 10-minute pitch, finishing just under the time limit.

The report, Spaceflight Passenger Safety Standard, is a proposal to an international spaceflight safety commission. It sets out a tiered standard covering medical emergencies, mental health crises and fatalities at every stage of a flight, backed by independent audits and a shared incident database.

The oral round was where we were strongest. We came in with clear talking points, answered the judges' questions on the spot, and walked out confident. Then they called our name at the awards ceremony.

My part

  • Presented the budget, risk, impact and recommendation sections of the pitch and handled judges' questions on them.
  • Co-wrote the report and built the case with Brandon and Aanya.

What it took

  • Research: regulation, accident history and medical risk, with 40+ cited sources.
  • Modelling: a likelihood × severity risk matrix and a five-year cost model.
  • Communication: a written proposal and a timed pitch, defended live.

Thanks to the McMaster Engineering Competition organising committee, and to Seif Otefa, our competition lead.

The problem

The market is growing. Passenger protection is not.

Space tourism market$8.9B → $62.1B

Who sets the rules?No one, yet

The FAA is barred from passenger safety rules until January 1, 2028. A signed risk acknowledgement is a passenger's only protection.

Before flight
  • No required medical exam
  • No check on flight day
  • No rule on who may fly
  • No mental health screening
During flight
  • Training covers only fire and cabin pressure loss
  • No plan for medical emergencies
  • 60–80% get motion sickness
  • No authority to restrain a passenger
After landing
  • No medical check at landing
  • No follow-up on recovery
  • Investigations led by the operator

Timing, trust and international plans

Timing

No commercial flight since June 2024; it returns in 2027 with new Delta-class ships, a natural start for a new protocol.

Trust

The 2014 SpaceShipTwo accident killed a crew member. Public certification helps rebuild confidence.

International

It is studying flights from Grottaglie, Italy: the ideal test case for FAA–ENAC mutual recognition.

  1. 2027Phase 1

    Delta returns. Adopt Tier S rules and submit the safety plan.

  2. NextPhase 2

    Pass the first audit and earn public certification.

  3. Jan 1, 2028Moratorium ends

    A ready model for governments.

  4. ThenPhase 3

    Extend to Italy through FAA–ENAC mutual recognition.

Recommendation. Adopt the Spaceflight Passenger Safety Standard before January 1, 2028. It covers medical, psychological and fatality risks at every stage of flight, scales by tier, is verified by independent audits, and starts with Virgin Galactic before growing through mutual recognition.
