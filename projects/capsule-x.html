Capsule X

A passive impact capsule for bringing fragile samples back from other worlds. No parachute and no propulsion: a triangulated aluminium lattice and layered thermal protection take the landing, so the sample doesn't.

The brief

Land hard, keep the sample perfect

As NASA returns fragile samples from celestial bodies at high velocity, it needs a passive, lattice-based impact capsule that absorbs extreme landing forces and delivers the samples intact, sealed and uncontaminated.

Sample
Must stay fully intact, with no damage at all.
Seal
No breaches and no contamination.
Passive only
No propulsion and no active mechanisms.
No deceleration aids
No parachutes or deployable decelerators.
How
Impact mitigation has to come from structure, material and geometry alone.
Design path

From springs to a lattice

Every concept kept the same idea: hold the sample canister at the centre and put as much energy-absorbing structure as possible between it and the ground.

Concept 1

Canister on springs

The first sketch suspends the sample canister on springs inside an egg-shaped shell, so the shell can decelerate while the springs soften the jolt.

Problem: springs store energy and give it back as rebound. The brief needed it absorbed.
Concept 2

Fill the shell with lattice

The canister sits inside a dense lattice that fills the shell. A lattice crushes instead of bouncing, so the energy goes into deforming struts.

Kept: the lattice as the main energy absorber.
Concept 3

Lattice core, braced frame

A lattice core around the canister, held inside a geodesic outer frame by radial struts. Two stages: the frame takes the first hit, the core takes what is left.

Kept: radial struts tying the core to the shell.
Detail

Designing the lattice cell

We sketched the triangular pattern to a 20 cm limit, then mirrored the original section to build out a symmetric layer, before modelling it in CAD.

Rule: every cell a triangle, so every strut works in tension or compression.
Final

Capsule X

The canister sits at the centre of a spherical lattice cage, tied to a layered outer shell by radial struts. The shell handles heat; the lattice handles the impact.

Result: podium finish at the CAD Designathon.
Why a lattice

Spread the hit over every strut

A triangulated lattice can't change shape without stretching or crushing its struts, so a load at one node is shared out through the whole structure instead of punching through. Click any node along the top to drop an impact there.

Triangulation

A triangle is the only polygon that can't deform without changing a side length. Built from triangles, the lattice is stiff and every strut carries pure tension or compression.

Load sharing

Each node passes load to several neighbours, so the force per strut drops as it travels toward the sample instead of concentrating in one path.

Absorb, don't bounce

Ductile struts can yield and crush progressively. That turns impact energy into permanent deformation of the lattice rather than rebound into the sample.
